01 The gap

GTFS Pathways describes what connects to what inside a transit station — stairs here, elevator there. It does not describe whether those connections are usable. A wheelchair user cannot see from the feed whether stairs become a dead end. A blind rider cannot see whether the elevator announces its position.

02 The approach

Pulled the live Sound Transit GTFS feed (SC-Fall-2026.5, 380 stops, 23 Link stations), fetched station footprint polygons and surface building roofs from the public ArcGIS REST services, synthesized pathways.txt where the feed lacked it, and enriched each pathway with eight ADAAG-anchored accessibility attributes. A Jupyter QA notebook replaced the original project's ArcGIS ModelBuilder chain and cut mapping time by ~30%.

03 The outcome

Four audited Link stations (Capitol Hill, UW, U District, Roosevelt). 24 barriers surfaced — 4 blocking, 5 moderate, 15 minor. The output GeoJSON drops into the Taskar Center's AccessMap routing tool with no schema change.

The 2D atlas opens on the full Link system — 23 stations with the real line geometry. The four audited stations show in red; click any to zoom into its footprint, surface buildings, and synthesized pathways color-coded by barrier severity.

Data sources

Sound Transit GTFS feed (SC-Fall-2026.5, 380 stops), Sound Transit ArcGIS REST services STArtMap/MapServer and STTransitService/MapServer, and OpenStreetMap Overpass as an optional supplement. Everything the pipeline reads is public.

Methods

Three stages: fetch (live GTFS + ArcGIS geometry), synthesize (generate pathways.txt entries where ST does not currently ship them), enrich (classify barrier severity from field-rule heuristics in src/enrich_gtfs.py:classify_severity). The QA notebook validates CRS, checks topology, flags duplicates, scores completeness, rolls up severity, and exports a QA report.

Why it matters for a transit agency

Transit agencies and civic-tech teams care about pipelines more than they care about maps. This one accepts a data/raw/field_audit.csv override keyed by pathway_id — the day a real audit CSV lands, the pipeline swaps synthetic for real with no code change.

GTFS Pathways gives stair_count, max_slope, length, width. That is not enough. These eight attributes extend it:

Field Why it matters ADAAG
has_handrail Stairs with 4+ risers need handrails on both sides §505
audible_signals Elevators must announce position §407.4
tactile_paving Platform edges need detectable warnings §705
braille_signage Permanent signs need braille §703.3
audible_wayfinding Entrances with voice announcement systems §707
lighting_quality Field-rated adequacy for low-vision riders (1–5) §215
tactile_warning_strip Detectable warning strip at platform edge §705.1
compliance_adaag Rolled-up pass/fail across the above —

Severity 0–3 is derived from these fields by rule set in src/enrich_gtfs.py:classify_severity. 0 = none, 1 = minor, 2 = moderate, 3 = blocking.

The original project (CSE 495, Sound Transit × Taskar Center, Sept 2023 – Mar 2024) collected real field audit data at three stations under an NDA that keeps the specific values out of a public repo. This replica preserves the pipeline, the schema, and the ADAAG anchoring verbatim, and substitutes deterministic synthetic values keyed by pathway_id. The pipeline accepts a data/raw/field_audit.csv override — if real values land, synthetic drops out automatically.
